# Neon Vice: Backstories

Lore bible for the three operators and McWey. It doesn't ship with the game. Everything here fits the lines already in `dialogue.js` and `index.html`; where the game shows a piece of it, the location is noted. Write new lines in the voices from `docs/STYLE-DIALOGUE.md` §3, and let the player find this out in pieces, never in a speech.

The common thread: **everybody McWey hires arrived with nothing, the way he did.** He lends them a car, takes 60%, calls them "kid" until they're square, exactly as the bell captain at the Grand Biscayne once did to him.

---

## McWey

**Desmond McWey.** Born 1931 in Miami Beach. Nobody calls him Desmond.

- **The Grand Biscayne.** His father was a doorman who died young. His mother scrubbed floors and did the sheets at the Grand Biscayne hotel. At fourteen Desmond was carrying bags there for Family men who tipped in silver dollars. The bell captain docked his uniform from his tips and called him "kid" for the fourteen months it took to pay it off. After that it was "Mr McWey".
- **Gold.** Paper money changed hands and lied about it; the silver dollars never did. "In Miami, gold doesn't lie. People do." is the first thing he ever believed.
- **Havana, 1959.** The Family sent him to Havana in the fifties to run the cage at one of their casinos. When the revolution came he packed the cage's gold into a suitcase and crossed ninety miles to Florida on a shrimp boat, sick the whole way (why he still gets seasick and won't go near Lex's boats). He handed back every bar. The Family has trusted him ever since and set him up running Miami for them. He answers to them, and never says so.
- **The view.** From the Grand Biscayne's roof, where his mother hung the sheets, you could see the lot where Coral Tower now stands. He meant to have its top floor. Moreau paid for it first.
- **McWey Tower.** Fifty-two floors of gold, built on a ground lease, which is how Dorian Kade ends up owning the block under it in Act 3 ("Everybody pays somebody rent"). The Grand Biscayne gala in Act 3 is Kade buying councilmen in the ballroom McWey's mother used to mop.
- **The shark.** Has eaten exactly one accountant: Morty Glass, who skimmed McWey for six years (see the Money Man).
- **His mother** is dead. "My mother would be so proud" about the gold plinth is half a joke.
- **Lex** was his "golden boy": flashy, earned well, never respected. Giving him Moreau's tower was business.

**In game:** optional questions "What view?" (Moreau brief), "You really get seasick?" (Royal Flush brief), "You know the Grand Biscayne?" (gala brief), "Who called you kid?" (the square-up scene). Open to every operator.

## The Don

**Salvatore "Sal" Benedetti.** Federal Hill, Providence. Born 1939.

- Third Don of the Benedetti family, after his grandfather and father.
- In 1985 his own capo wore a wire. The federal case put the family in prison; Sal did eighteen months on a weapons charge.
- He came out to a seized house, frozen accounts, and one thing the government missed: his father's .357, wrapped in oilcloth and buried in his mother's tomato bed. It's the gun he starts with.
- He pawned his real gold chain to pay the lawyer and wears a plated copy until he can buy the original back. Lex's crack about the chain "turning your neck green" lands because it's true.
- He drove south because Miami has no one family. Working for McWey is a step, not a ceiling. "This city answers to one family" is a plan.
- McWey sent flowers to Providence when the family went down.
- Voice: fewest words in the room. Statements, never questions. Doesn't joke about money.

**In game:** select-screen file; brief 0 "You knew my father."; finale "I'm starting a family."; one Tony line.

## The Consigliere

**Augusto "Gus" Ferrante.** South Philadelphia. Born 1944.

- Golden Gloves heavyweight in 1963 (the size and the toughness), then law school at night.
- Consigliere to old Don Carmine Lo Bianco. The day Gus passed the bar, the Don gave him the gold knuckle dusters.
- In 1984 Gus advised the Don to accept a peace meeting at a diner on Passyunk Avenue. It was an ambush. The Don died in the booth. Gus took a bullet in the vest he always wore under the suit (the perk) and put two men through the diner window with the dusters.
- The family decided a consigliere who walks out of an ambush alive is either lucky or paid. Out of respect for the Don's widow they exiled him instead of killing him, and took his name with his job. Lex's "did you pawn the family name?" hits exactly there.
- He still gives advice. Now he gives it with his fists, and he's always polite about it.
- At the end, the widow writes to McWey: four pages of apologies. Philadelphia wants him back.
- Voice: polite words, violent meaning. Never swears, never rushes.

**In game:** select-screen file; brief 0 "You'll want my advice as well."; finale "Philadelphia called me."; one Tony line.

## The Money Man

**Julian "Jules" Pryce.** Brickell, by way of Wharton. Born 1953.

- Wharton class of '77. Came to Miami in 1979, when its banks were drowning in cash nobody could explain.
- Junior partner to Morty Glass, the CPA who kept the books for half the city, McWey included.
- Jules found Morty's second set of books: six years of skimming from McWey, and the shortfall quietly pinned on Jules. He carried them up to the fifty-second floor himself. He didn't think McWey would feed Morty to the shark. McWey's first line to every new hire ("he's only ever eaten one accountant") means something very specific to Jules.
- The Feds, already circling Glass & Pryce, froze every dollar he had. He kept a white suit and a black book with everybody's price in it. That book is the perk: he knows what every guard costs and where every one of them is standing.
- He's never been punched, and it shows (toughness 1). He's fast because he's always been the one leaving the room early.
- Lex was a client who hated him; the quarter Lex tossed him at Club Flamingo was a tip.
- At the end McWey makes a phone call and the accounts unfreeze, a bonus that never shows up in the books.
- Voice: fast and transactional, always pricing something. No sentiment.

**In game:** select-screen file; brief 0 "About Morty."; finale "The Feds unfroze my accounts."; one Tony line.
